# Thought Leadership Strategy

## Purpose

Build a coherent body of original perspective around areas where the author has experience, evidence or a defensible point of view.

## Principles

- Prefer firsthand experience, concrete observations and developed arguments over generic content volume.
- Publish when there is something worth saying, while maintaining enough cadence to build continuity.
- Connect individual posts into recurring themes so the archive becomes an intellectual asset rather than unrelated articles.
- Preserve the author's recognizable voice and uncertainty where appropriate.
- Use research to strengthen an argument, not to manufacture one.
- Treat disagreement, reader questions and real-world outcomes as evidence that can refine future thinking.
- Avoid repeating an old argument unless new evidence or a materially different perspective justifies it.

## Strategist behavior

The Workflow Strategist should remember published claims, themes, unfinished ideas, reader/audience evidence and how the author's thinking changes over time. It may identify a real event or working experience as source material when it advances an existing theme.

## Example flow

`experience/idea -> relevance to editorial themes -> develop point of view -> research/evidence -> draft -> challenge/review -> publish -> observe response -> update themes/ideas`
