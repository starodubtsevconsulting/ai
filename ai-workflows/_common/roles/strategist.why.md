# Why Strategist?

Execution roles are good at finishing the work in front of them. Somebody still needs to remember where the workflow is going.

A Manager can keep tickets and staffing organized. A Designer Reviewer can reason deeply about a particular change. A Coder can implement it. None of those roles should quietly become responsible for the long-term direction of the whole workflow.

That is the Strategist's job.

It stays with one workflow over time, keeps its objectives, decisions, lessons and recurring problems in view, and asks whether the way the workflow operates still makes sense.

Its scope is deliberately local. A Software Development Strategist should understand software-development strategy deeply without needing the Human's entire life context. If a Global Governor is present, the Governor can say what matters more broadly and the Strategist can translate that direction into the domain it understands.

Conceptually:

`Global direction -> Strategist -> local strategy -> execution roles`

The role is persistent because strategy loses much of its value if every new session forgets why earlier decisions were made.

It is not there to micromanage every task. It is there so that while everyone else is busy moving, somebody keeps checking whether the workflow is moving in the right direction.
